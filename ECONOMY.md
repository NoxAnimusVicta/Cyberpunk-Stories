# Campaign economic reference

Draft 1, 31 October 2026. Proposed Night City opening-year economy, provisionally 2078. Prices are eurodollars, written eb or €$. They are fictional campaign accounting values, not real-world exchange rates. Nothing here purchases equipment or gives the protagonist income. This reference supports street survival, travel, property and businesses on one consistent scale.

## Evidence and pricing method

RED's economic article describes 2045 scarcity, monthly living costs and comparison-based pricing. It gives examples such as 10 eb cheap goods and 500 eb expensive goods. These are useful historical anchors, not a conversion formula for the 2070s. [Publisher explanation](https://rtalsoriangames.com/2020/11/06/cyberpunk-red-alert-the-economy/).

The official housing supplement lists cube hotels at 500 eb/month and containers at 1,000 eb/month in 2045. The campaign retains those two recognizable anchors by choice, rather than claiming no economic change occurred. [Cargo Containers and Cube Hotels, page 2](https://rtalsoriangames.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/RTG-CPR-CargoContainersandCubeHotels.pdf).

The Edgerunners Mission Kit explicitly bridges RED to 2070s technology and economy. The preview confirms that scope but does not provide its complete price tables. This draft does not claim to reproduce the purchased kit or the videogame's changing shop prices. [Official preview, page 7](https://rtalsoriangames.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/CEMK-PAX-U-2023-Preview.pdf).

Status keys: **A** = verified published price used as an anchor, with its original era stated; **E** = authored campaign estimate. All remaining values, including wages, property yields and regional adjustments, are E. They are designed to be usable together and remain reviewable before play. A range describes market variation; the default is the amount to use for an ordinary matching purchase. Exceptional quotes must identify why they differ.

## Work and contracts

Monthly wages below are take-home pay for a normal full-time schedule. Employer payroll cost is 1.25 times take-home for the business examples; that is an authored aggregate allowance, not a universal tax law. Housing benefits must be recorded separately. Unemployed people are not guaranteed a job at a listed wage.

Default fixer fee: 15% of gross contracted pay when the fixer arranged the job and the quote has not already included it. Quote crew total, individual shares, advance, fee and reimbursable expenses before acceptance. A four-person job paying 4,000 each is a 16,000 crew budget, not 4,000 divided four ways. Failed collection, reputation and client capacity affect available work; a powerful protagonist does not automatically receive infinite high-paying gigs.

For income property, use annual net operating income divided by a justified yield as a cross-check, not a magic universal rule. Proposed ordinary yield band: 6–10%; high-risk sites need 10–18% or a reason the buyer accepts less. Example: studio rent 1,400 × 12 = 16,800; 8% vacancy allowance 1,344; maintenance/insurance/fees 3,000; net 12,456. At a 7% yield, value ≈177,943, supporting the rounded 180,000 estimate. An assessed price is not an offer to buy instantly.

Financing proposal for a legally eligible buyer: 30% deposit, individually quoted interest, term and collateral. Illustration only: 126,000 borrowed for 20 years at 9% nominal annual interest costs about 1,134/month. That exceeds the example's average monthly operating income of 1,038: leverage does not create free profit. A fugitive with frozen accounts does not automatically qualify.

## Businesses and cash flow

All E. Values below assume leased premises. Buying a building is separate. Start-up cash includes fit-out, equipment, initial stock and the stated operating reserve; no second charge for those same assets.

| Business | Start-up range eb | Default planning budget eb | Reserve within budget |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Food kiosk | 8,000–25,000 | 15,000 | 3 months fixed costs |
| Small repair workshop | 25,000–70,000 | 40,000 | 3 months fixed costs |
| Small bar/café | 60,000–160,000 | 100,000 | 3 months fixed costs |
| Licensed small clinic | 120,000–400,000 | 220,000 | 6 months fixed costs |
| Private specialist research lab | 500,000–3,000,000 | 1,200,000 | 6 months fixed costs |

Workshop example: 120 jobs/month × 120 average invoice = 14,400 revenue. Parts 3,600; employed technician 3,500 take-home ×1.25 = 4,375 employer cost; rent 1,200; utilities 250; insurance/licences 400; maintenance 350; security 300. Operating surplus = 3,925 before owner labour, financing and tax. Reserve 20% of surplus for campaign-estimated business tax: 785; remainder 3,140. This is modest owner-operated income, not a passive 118,000/year profit machine. A hired manager, slower demand or a loan can eliminate it. Break-even before owner compensation/tax: 6,875 fixed costs ÷90 contribution/job ≈77 jobs/month.

Business purchase price proposal: recoverable operating assets plus 2–4 years of verified maintainable earnings after a market wage for the owner's labour, then subtract assumed debt. Distressed businesses can be worth less than their equipment. Do not value revenue as profit or add the same working capital twice. Research companies may have negative cash flow and require milestone financing; a valuable idea is not cash until someone pays.

Use these E comparisons for initial quotes, not permanent regional stereotypes. They affect local service, rent and ordinary wage baskets separately from global equipment. Research a destination more closely before a consequential transaction there.

| Isolated frontier or disrupted corridor | Quote locally | Quote locally | Freight and availability, not a single multiplier |

Within-city class differences can exceed these ranges. A rich enclave is not priced as the surrounding poor district. Convert any local-currency quote into eb at a recorded fictional exchange rate at the transaction time; do not invent modern real-world rates.

Store base-year purchasing-power values and date each nominal quote. Review wages, food, rent, imported goods, energy and credit separately each story year or after a relevant shock. No automatic 100-year 2% inflation curve. If a scenario uses 2% for ten years, its factor is 1.02^10 ≈1.219; record the assumption and stop it when conditions change. Productivity may lower hardware costs while rents rise. Nothing adjusts an already signed fixed-price contract retroactively.

## Transaction record

Every actual commitment records event ID, story date, seller/buyer, item or service, quantity, currency, unit price, fee/tax, payment status, delivery, ownership and location. Separate cash, frozen funds, deposits, debt, earmarks and receivables. Balance check: opening balance + receipts − payments = closing balance. Quotes and expected income do not enter spendable cash.
